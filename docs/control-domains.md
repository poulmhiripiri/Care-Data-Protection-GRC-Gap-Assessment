# Control Domains

## 1. Governance and accountability

Objective: Ensure the care provider can demonstrate compliance and ownership of data protection risk.

Example controls:

- Named data protection lead or DPO where required.
- Senior management oversight of data protection and cyber risk.
- Data protection policy approved and reviewed annually.
- Evidence of staff training.
- Compliance risk register reviewed regularly.

## 2. Records of processing

Objective: Understand what personal data is processed, why, where it is stored and who it is shared with.

Example controls:

- Record of Processing Activities exists.
- Lawful basis recorded for each processing activity.
- Article 9 special category condition recorded where health/care data is processed.
- Data flows mapped for major systems.

## 3. Special category data

Objective: Ensure sensitive health/care information receives additional protection.

Example controls:

- Health and care data identified and classified.
- Access is role-based and restricted.
- Staff understand confidentiality obligations.
- Secure transfer methods are used.

## 4. Transparency

Objective: Ensure service users, relatives, staff and visitors understand how their information is used.

Example controls:

- Privacy notices are accessible, layered and written clearly.
- Notices explain data sharing with councils, NHS, GP, pharmacy, emergency services and regulators.
- Staff privacy notice exists.
- CCTV notice exists where CCTV is used.

## 5. Individual rights

Objective: Ensure rights requests are handled consistently and within legal timescales.

Example controls:

- SAR process documented.
- Requests logged and tracked.
- Staff know how to identify and escalate requests.
- Exemptions and third-party data checks are documented.

## 6. DPIA and privacy by design

Objective: Ensure high-risk processing is assessed before implementation.

Example controls:

- DPIA screening is performed for new systems and projects.
- DPIA register is maintained.
- Risks are reviewed by data protection lead/DPO.
- Mitigations are tracked to closure.

## 7. Data sharing

Objective: Ensure personal data sharing is lawful, documented, proportionate and secure.

Example controls:

- Sharing partners identified.
- Data sharing agreements exist where appropriate.
- Lawful basis is documented before sharing.
- Secure sharing channels are used.

## 8. Supplier and processor management

Objective: Ensure third parties process care-provider data securely and contractually.

Example controls:

- Supplier register exists.
- Processor contracts include UK GDPR clauses.
- Due diligence checks are completed.
- Exit and deletion arrangements are documented.

## 9. Information security

Objective: Protect personal data from unauthorised access, loss, damage or compromise.

Example controls:

- MFA is enabled for email, cloud systems and remote access.
- User access is reviewed periodically.
- Devices are encrypted.
- Backups are tested.
- Endpoint protection is deployed.

## 10. Vulnerability and patch governance

Objective: Reduce exploitable technical risk affecting care systems and personal data.

Example controls:

- Asset inventory exists.
- Vulnerability scans are scheduled.
- Patching SLAs are defined.
- Critical vulnerabilities are remediated or formally risk accepted.
- External attack surface is reviewed.

## 11. Breach and incident response

Objective: Ensure incidents are detected, assessed, escalated and learned from.

Example controls:

- Breach response procedure exists.
- Incidents are logged.
- ICO reporting decision is documented.
- Lessons learned are implemented.

## 12. Retention and disposal

Objective: Ensure personal data is kept only as long as required and securely disposed of.

Example controls:

- Retention schedule exists.
- Paper records are securely stored and destroyed.
- Digital deletion process is documented.
- Archive access is restricted.
